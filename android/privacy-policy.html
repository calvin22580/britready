Privacy Policy

Life in UK Practice for Android · Last updated: 16 November 2026

This policy explains how the Life in UK Practice app for Android phones and tablets ("the app"), called "Practice: Life in UK Test" (followed by the year) on Google Play, handles your information. The iPhone and iPad app has its own privacy policy. In short, the app collects no personal data unless you choose to give it: it sends anonymous usage counts, which you can turn off, and your email address only if you ask for a question a day by email.

Who we are

The app is published by Steady Lamp Ltd, a company registered in England and Wales, company number 17533499, which is responsible for the personal data described here. Registered office: 128 City Road, London EC1V 2NX. Contact: support@steadylamp.com.

Information we collect

The app has no user accounts and doesn't send your answers, progress or settings to us or to anyone else, apart from Google Play as described under "Purchases and subscriptions". It shows no ads and doesn't track you across other companies' apps or websites.

Anonymous usage counts

The app sends anonymous counts of how it is used, so we can see which features help and fix what doesn't. These are events such as "app opened", "practice started" or "mock test finished" (with the kind of practice, the number of questions and the score), "purchase screen shown", "plan bought" (weekly, monthly or lifetime), and changes to the help language. Each comes with the app and Android versions, whether it's a phone or a tablet, the language you chose for the notes and your phone's main language (just the language, such as "English", not your region), and a random id made by the app on your device, which isn't linked to you, your Google Account or your device's own identifiers, and is removed if you uninstall the app. They also say which link the app was installed from, as Google Play reports it: the name of an advert's campaign (such as "chatgpt"), or that you found the app in Google Play. That is a label for the advert, not for you.

They don't include your name, email, location, answers to individual questions, test date or anything you type. We don't use them for advertising, don't build profiles of people and don't sell or share them.

They are processed for us by PostHog Inc. (posthog.com), on servers in the United States, under the UK's data protection safeguards for international transfers. PostHog is set not to store IP addresses or work out a location. We keep the counts for up to 12 months. Our basis is our legitimate interest in improving the app (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).

From version 1.6, if the app crashes while "Share anonymous usage data" is on, the app saves a crash report on your device and, the next time you open it, sends it through PostHog (as above) with the same anonymous details: the kind of crash, a short technical message, the app and Android version, and a list of the places in the app's code where it happened. It holds nothing you typed or answered and no name, email or test date. With "Share anonymous usage data" off, no crash report is saved or sent. We keep crash reports for up to 12 months, to find and fix the problem. Our basis is our legitimate interest in keeping the app working (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).

To turn them off, go to Settings → Privacy in the app and turn off "Share anonymous usage data". Nothing more is sent from then on.

From version 1.6, when you buy Full Access while "Share anonymous usage data" is on, the app gives Google Play the same random id with the purchase, so Google Play's notices to us about renewals, cancelled free trials and refunds can be matched to the anonymous counts. It isn't sent with purchases while that setting is off.

From version 1.6, if you write something in the app's feedback box and tap Send, the text is sent to us through PostHog (as above), with the same anonymous details as the usage counts, such as the app version, so we can improve the app. If "Share anonymous usage data" is off, it goes with a one-off random id instead of this install's, so it can't be joined to anything else. It isn't linked to your name or email, so we can't reply; please don't include personal details. It is sent only when you tap Send, whether or not "Share anonymous usage data" is on. We keep feedback for up to 12 months. Our basis is our legitimate interest in improving the app (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).

Measuring our Google ads

From version 1.6, if "Share anonymous usage data" is on, the app also tells Google Analytics for Firebase (Google LLC) when it is first opened and when Full Access is bought, with the plan and price. Google's software also records basic app events by itself, such as when the app is opened (sessions), how long it is used, and app and system updates, with the device model and app and system versions. This lets Google tell us how many people who found the app through our Google ads went on to install it and buy, so we can stop paying for ads that don't work. Google receives a random id made by its software for this install of the app, how the app was installed from Google Play, and your IP address, from which it works out an approximate location (city level). It doesn't receive your name, email, answers, test date or your device's advertising ID. The app isn't linked to other apps' data. Google processes this under its terms for Google Analytics for Firebase and may store it outside the UK under the UK's data protection safeguards for international transfers. The data isn't used to personalise ads. Google keeps it for 2 months for exploration reports, and we keep the totals. Our basis is our legitimate interest in knowing which advertising works (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). Turning off "Share anonymous usage data" stops this too.

Question of the day by email

From version 1.6, Settings has a switch, "Email me a question a day". It is off unless you turn it on, and the app never asks for your email address anywhere else. If you turn it on and enter your email address, the app sends the address to our server and we email you a link to confirm it is yours. Once you confirm, we send one practice question a day to that address. We use the address only for this. We don't use it for advertising by anyone else, and we don't sell or share it. It isn't joined to the anonymous usage counts or to your progress, which stays on your device.

Our basis is your consent (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(a)), which you can withdraw at any time: use the unsubscribe link in any of the emails, turn the switch off in Settings, or email us. We keep your address while you're subscribed. If you unsubscribe, or never confirm, we delete it within 30 days. The address is stored by Supabase (in the European Union) and the emails are sent by Resend (from servers in the European Union); both act only on our behalf. The emails come from hello@lifeintheukquestions.co.uk, the address of our website, Life in the UK Questions, which runs the same service.

If you choose to email us, for example with "Report a problem" under a question, your email app sends us your message, your email address and the details it shows (such as the question's ID and the app and Android versions). We use them only to reply and to fix the question, and we don't share them.

Information stored on your device

To work, the app saves the following only on your device:

  • your answers, study progress, saved questions and mock test results, and how your real test went if you tell the app;
  • your settings, such as your test date, reminder time and haptics preference;
  • whether you have Full Access.

This data stays on your device, apart from the anonymous counts above and your progress (everything in the first point above, and your test date) being included in Android's backup to your Google Account, and in a transfer to a new phone, if you use them. Your other settings and your purchase status aren't included. You can clear your progress at any time with Settings → Reset all progress in the app (it keeps your test date and how your real test went), or remove everything from your phone by uninstalling the app. Android's backup is kept in your Google Account, under your own backup settings.

Purchases and subscriptions

In-app purchases and subscriptions, including renewals, cancellations and refunds, are handled by Google through Google Play. Neither the app nor we receive your payment details. The app uses Google Play Billing, Google's library for purchases, to ask Google Play what you've bought, only to unlock Full Access; the library may also send Google technical information about purchase requests (diagnostics). When the app asks whether you'd like to rate it, the rating window is Google Play's, and the app never sees your rating or review. Google gives us sales reports (for example the order number, what was bought, the price and the country), which we use for our accounts and, if you ask us, to help with a purchase. Google's handling of all this is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

Notifications

If you turn on daily reminders, the app schedules them locally on your device with Android's alarms. No push notification service is used and no data is sent anywhere.

Why we use your data and how long we keep it

  • Your email address, if you ask for a question a day: to send you the emails, with your consent. We keep it while you're subscribed and delete it within 30 days of you unsubscribing (or of signing up, if you never confirm).
  • Emails you send us: to answer you and fix any problem you report, which is in our legitimate interests (and yours). We keep a conversation for up to 12 months after it ends, then delete it.
  • Google's sales reports: to keep our business accounts, which the law requires (legal obligation). We keep them for as long as HMRC requires, generally at least 5 years after the 31 January filing deadline for the tax year of the sale.

Your rights

You can ask to see the personal data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, or object to our using it: email us at support@steadylamp.com. Everything else stays on your device, under your control. You also have the right to complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

Children

The app is a general study aid and doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the "last updated" date above. If we ever start collecting more, we'll update the policy and the app's Data safety section on Google Play before doing so.
